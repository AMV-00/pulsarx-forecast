The NASA challenge we chose

Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions

Help is far away

On a Mars mission, a message to Earth takes up to 22 minutes one way. The crew must catch problems on their own.

Fatigue is a known risk

NASA lists sleep loss and a broken body clock among the risks of human spaceflight.

We can build it

Wearables, data and AI are ready today. The missing piece is software that turns them into a decision.

Source: NASA Human Research Program, Risks of Human Spaceflight

The problem

~6 h

of sleep a night in space, on average.

NASA schedules 8.5 h. And 3 in 4 ISS crew members took sleep medication.

The gap

Today fatigue is noticed after it happens. Nothing forecasts it against tomorrow’s critical tasks, like a spacewalk.

Why it matters

A tired crew makes more mistakes. On Earth the same gap hurts pilots, doctors and shift workers.

Source: Barger et al., The Lancet Neurology, 2014 (85 astronauts, Shuttle and ISS)

Impact

Why it is realistic

Smart rings already exist. FORECAST is software: it runs onboard, works offline, has 48 automated tests and open code.
